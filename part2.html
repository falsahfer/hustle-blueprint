The one rule that keeps quality high: automate the reps, never the relationship or the judgment.

An agent can draft ten posts before you finish your coffee. What it cannot do is have taste, hold a real relationship, or own the promise your name makes. Hand it the volume. Keep the moments that need a human. Get that line right and you produce five streams of content that still sound like you.

The Framework

Where it actually matters

The whole game is one line: what you hand to the agent, and what you keep for yourself. Get this split right and automation raises your quality. Get it wrong and you flood the world with soulless drafts. Here is the line, drawn clearly.

✓ Let the agent run it
  • First drafts, at volumeTwenty post ideas, five subject lines, a full outline. A blank page is where you stall. The agent never faces one.
  • Research and summarizingThe week's news in your niche, a long report boiled down, the stat you half-remember, pulled and cited.
  • Repurposing across formatsOne newsletter into eight tweets, a thread, and three short scripts. Same idea, every shape.
  • Formatting and cleanupFix the markdown, tighten to length, add the alt text, build the HTML. The boring last mile.
  • Monitoring and sortingWatch mentions, flag the replies worth answering, sort the inbox into "needs a human" and "ignore."
★ Keep your hands on it
  • The promiseWhat your offer actually delivers is your call, never a model's best guess. This is the thing people pay for.
  • Taste and the final cutThe agent drafts ten, you pick and sharpen the one. Editing is where your voice actually lives.
  • Real relationshipsThe note to a new subscriber, the reply to a paying member. People can always tell when it wasn't you.
  • Your point of viewThe take only you have, from work only you did. That is the entire reason anyone follows you.
  • The final yesNothing ships without your eyes on it. You own every word that goes out under your name.
"Where it matters" is not everywhere — and not nowhere.

The trap is thinking it's all or nothing: either automate everything and sound like a robot, or automate nothing and drown. The real move is surgical. Agents take the roughly 80% that is reps and research. You spend the hours you win back on the 20% that only you can do — the taste, the relationships, the actual idea. Quality climbs, because you finally have time for the parts that need you.

Agent Stacks · Streams 1–3

Agents for the free-to-start three

Affiliate, newsletter, and X. For each: the job you hand the agent, the part you keep, the stack plus a prompt you can steal today, and your one move this week.

1

Affiliate products

agent saves you
~3 hrs / week
What the agent handles

Drafts honest use-case posts for each tool, tracks which links get clicks, drafts your monthly "my stack" update, and scouts new tools worth a test.

Where it matters — you keep this

You actually use the tool and vouch for it. An agent can spend trust but never earn it. What you recommend is always your call.

The stack + a prompt to steal

A research agent plus a draft agent. "Here's how I use [tool] and the exact result I got. Draft 3 honest posts, no hype, and make one lead with a specific number."

Your move this week

Give an agent your 3 tools and last month's click data. Have it draft one honest post per tool. You publish only the ones that are true.

2

Newsletter

agent saves you
~4 hrs / issue
What the agent handles

Builds the outline from your week's notes, drafts the sections, writes 5 subject-line options, repurposes the issue into X posts, and proofreads.

Where it matters — you keep this

The one real story or lesson at the center. The agent assembles; you supply the thing worth reading. And the final edit for voice is yours.

The stack + a prompt to steal

A draft agent fed your rough notes plus 3 past issues for voice. "Match the voice in these issues. Draft from my bullets. Flag anything you invented so I can check it."

Your move this week

Dump this week's rough notes into an agent. Have it return an outline and first draft. You rewrite the intro in your own words, then send.

3

X / Twitter

agent saves you
~5 hrs / week
What the agent handles

Drafts a week of posts from your seed ideas, queues them in a scheduler, watches your niche for reply-worthy threads, and drafts reply options.

Where it matters — you keep this

The actual reply in someone's mentions, and any real take. Automated replies read as automated. Show up as a person where it counts.

The stack + a prompt to steal

An idea-to-draft agent plus a monitor. "From these 5 ideas, draft 15 posts in my voice. Then list 5 bigger accounts posting about X today that are worth a real reply."

Your move this week

Feed an agent 5 seed ideas, have it draft next week's posts. You approve and schedule the batch, but write every reply yourself.

Same pattern every time: agent drafts the volume, you keep the taste and the replies. Page 3 of 5.
Agent Stacks · Streams 4–5 + The Loop

Agents for the high-leverage two

Community and the core hustle move the most money, so the human part matters most here. Then the quality-control loop that ties every stream together and keeps the output sounding like you.

4

Private paid community

agent saves you
~3 hrs / week
What the agent handles

Drafts daily prompts to keep the room alive, summarizes the week's best threads into a recap, drafts onboarding messages, and flags members who went quiet.

Where it matters — you keep this

Being actually present. People pay to be closer to you, not your bot. Show up live and answer real questions in your own words.

The stack + a prompt to steal

A recap agent run on your chat export. "Summarize this week's chat into a 5-bullet recap and 3 discussion prompts for next week. Keep my casual tone."

Your move this week

Automate the weekly recap and a daily prompt. Then block 15 real minutes a day to reply as yourself. That presence is the product.

5

Scale the core hustle

agent saves you
the setup grind
What the agent handles

Drafts sales-page copy variants, pulls testimonials from your community and replies, researches competitor pricing, and drafts the FAQ and onboarding.

Where it matters — you keep this

The offer itself and the promise you can keep. Pricing is your judgment call. Delivery is you. The agent stages it; you decide it.

The stack + a prompt to steal

A copy agent fed your best client result and real testimonials. "Draft 3 sales headlines around this result. No superlatives. Lead with the number."

Your move this week

Have an agent assemble your testimonials and draft 3 headline options. You pick the true one, then raise your price on the next buyer.

The loop is the point, not the tool.

Agents are fast, but they have no taste and no relationships. Keep yourself at the start of every loop (the idea) and the end (the edit and the reply). Automate the messy middle. Do that and you produce five streams' worth of content at a quality one person could never hit by hand.

Money streams need more of you, not less. Agents free up the hours to give it. Page 4 of 5.
Rollout + Your Agent Plan

Your first 90 days with a crew

Do not automate all five streams on day one — you will lose your voice and not notice until it's gone. Add agents gradually, teach each one how you sound, then fill in the canvas with your own plan.

The 90-Day Agent Rollout — teach the crew before you trust it

Days 1–30 · Teach
  • Pick one agent tool, learn to prompt it well
  • Feed it 3 past pieces so it learns your voice
  • Automate one thing: newsletter into X posts
  • Write everything else by hand for now
    • Days 31–60 · Expand
  • Add a research agent for newsletter prep
  • Let an agent draft your week of X posts
  • Set up monitoring for mentions + threads
  • You still write every reply and final edit
    • Days 61–90 · Systemize
  • One agent per stream, each with a voice prompt
  • A weekly QC block: edit + approve the batch
  • Automate community recaps + affiliate drafts
  • Audit: would you have written it? If not, retune
